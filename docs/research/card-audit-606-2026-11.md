# What the session card carries today, November 2026 (#606, first stage)

Status: **static audit, measured 2026-11-02.** It counts what the current code delivers. It does not say whether a
smaller card makes answers better or worse: there was no A/B on real prompts, and no fact was labelled by meaning.
#606 stays open.

A later [classification and exploratory replay](card-replay-606-2026-11.md) reports 157 labelled facts and the
24-request comparison, including its unresolved outcomes. The limitations above describe this first stage only;
repository letters are local to each study and must not be joined across the two notes.

## Method

- **Code**: commit `8ed3b53`, binary `0.26.1-r601.8ed3b53` built from it. Nothing in the code changed.
- **Base**: a fresh private copy of the text history used for #598. The binary ran only on the copy, with its own
  `MEMCUE_STATE_DIR`, a fresh `HOME`, no global or system git config, an empty `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` and
  `MEMCUE_DREAM_BACKGROUND=0`. That isolates the files the binary reads and writes; it is not a network or process
  sandbox. The source snapshot and the binary hashed the same before and after the run.
- **The copy after opening**: opening the copy rewrote 60 of its 7,049 files. The changes are in the records, not
  the format. In the latest version of a fact, `author` changed in 60 files, `queries` in 57 and `scope` in 3; across
  all versions, `queries` in 253 versions, `author` in 120, `scope` in 6 and `source` in 2. No file gained or lost a
  version. A possible mechanism, not proven: the open merges each file's history with the cache's
  (`bringTogether`, `internal/store/textstore.go`), and `MergeHistories` (`internal/store/merge.go`) puts diverged
  tails in a canonical order and renumbers them. A synthetic probe with a fresh cache changed the latest scope and
  author and kept both versions. All counts and metadata below are of the copy **after** the open.
- **Contexts**: three public repositories, A, B and C, assigned to letters at random on each run. The fourth context,
  G, is a directory outside git, which gets the global tier only.
- **Claude Code**: `session-start -part 1`, `-part 2` and `-part 3`, the three hooks of the plugin. Stated facts get a
  room of 3 × 9,500 characters, and the other facts share the 3,500-character budget (`renderCardWith`).
- **Hermes**: `session-card -dream-tools` with the same `cwd`. Every fact shares the 3,500-character budget.
- **Counting**: the fact ids and the pool counters come from `card.log`, the lines from the delivered
  `additionalContext`, kind, scope and source from the copy's cache. The script fails when the logged ids and the
  delivered lines differ in number.
- **Metric**: *line cost* is the length of a fact line plus one newline, the same for every line. It is a normalized
  cost, not the physical text: a part ends without its last newline. *Delivered* is the physical length of the
  `additionalContext` of all parts. *Pool* is carried plus left out by budget plus cut past part 3.
- **Script**: `docs/research/scripts/card_audit_606.py`, synthetic tests in `test_card_audit_606.py`. It checks
  every input and output before it creates the scratch: no links or special files in the snapshot, a regular
  executable binary, three distinct clones, a new public file outside the snapshot and the scratch. Public names of
  kind, scope and source come from fixed lists; any other value counts as `other`. Public counts are in
  `card-audit-606-2026-11.json`. Ids, texts and the mapping of letters to repositories stay in a private scratch
  directory.

## The mechanical trim

The trim keeps the facts with `source` `user` or `user_confirmed`. It is a **filter by source and scope, not a
classifier of standing behaviour**: a stated fact may be knowledge, and an agent's fact may be a rule the user gave.

| Claude Code | before | after | line cost removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 67 / 11,390 | 46 / 8,235 | 28 % |
| B | 72 / 13,828 | 54 / 10,705 | 23 % |
| C | 122 / 22,430 | 109 / 21,094 | 6 % |
| G | 64 / 11,217 | 44 / 7,967 | 29 % |

On Hermes the trim removes 0 to 2 facts. The trimmed Claude Code card still costs 8 to 21 thousand characters. The
39 stated global rules alone cost 6,943 on every card, three times the 2,161 characters of the 14 behaviour facts the
issue reports. So a trim by source does not reach the card the issue describes; that card needs a selection by
meaning, which this stage did not make.

## Why the selection by meaning is not measured

- No fact was labelled. A private packet of the unique carried facts (id, text, kind, scope, source, the cards that
  carried each one and its position) was prepared for labelling outside this run. Nobody has looked at it yet.
- No real prompt was run. This audit did not select a matched real-prompt sample or construct a semantically
  labelled trimmed card. Saved local research corpora contain real prompts; their existence is not evidence that
  this stage performed the A/B. Synthetic prompts alone would not establish what real sessions lose.

## How this differs from the issue's measurement

The issue reports one repository of the author on a copy of the author's base: 87 facts, 13,103 characters of fact
text, 26 agent decisions of the repository, and 14 behaviour facts by one reader's labels. That is a prior result;
this note did not repeat it. Here:

- The repositories are different: three public ones and a directory outside git.
- The base is the #598 copy after the open. Whether it already has the relabels of #600 and #607 was not checked.
- The cost here is the normalized line cost, `- ` and one newline included, not the fact text alone.
- The card code is the same: #607 changed the dream and the note, not `CardFacts` or `renderCardWith`.
- At most 7 agent decisions of a repository are on a card here, against 26 in the issue.

The facts shared by all three cards are a hypothesis about where a cut would save the most. They say nothing about
what the cut does to answers.

## The four questions

1. **Does a trimmed card make answers better, the same or worse?** UNKNOWN. This needs the A/B on real prompts.
2. **A flag, a dream proposal or a new kind?** Not answered. The code shows what each one would touch:
   - A flag at write time works the way `-source user` works today: the model chooses at `add`, the card trusts it,
     and the stated facts already get their own room. It needs a new field in the store and in `add`. The class
     stays small only if the model uses the flag sparingly, and that has not been measured.
   - A dream proposal fits the dream's scope task of #607: the dream proposes, `-apply` checks. It costs model calls,
     and the user has to confirm each proposal.
   - A new kind changes `CardFacts`, the `-kind` filter of search, the dream's kind rules and the existing facts. It
     touches the most code.
   Which source a fact gets stays the model's choice, as the user decided; none of the options adds a check of who
   the author is.
3. **Should the agent's repository decisions leave the card?** Answered for cost only. The agent's repository
   decisions are 7 facts / 1,274 on A, 1 / 277 on B and 3 / 261 on C. Counting the agent's repository rules and the
   tool's decisions too, they are 8 / 1,607, 5 / 1,575 and 3 / 261. The cards also carry 10 to 13 global rules of the
   agent. What leaving the card does to answers is UNKNOWN.
4. **Which command and file facts does the program hook already serve?** UNKNOWN. This stage did not probe the hook.
   The local path matches of #598 are partial metadata that varies between runs, and they do not show which facts
   the hook would serve.

## Decision

No change to the card, its budget or its defaults until the A/B. To finish #606:

1. Label the private packet.
2. Run the A/B on real prompts of the author's repositories.

Which of the three ways a fact joins the card is the user's decision, not a result of this note.
